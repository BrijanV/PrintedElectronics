# Printed Flexible Force Sensor using Liquid Metal Electronics

The project focuses on the design, fabrication, assembly, and testing of a **flexible, stretchable force sensor** using printed liquid metal conductors and a flexible PCB.

---

## Project Overview

The objective of this lab was to design and fabricate a **soft, low-profile force sensor** capable of measuring **normal (vertical) force**. The sensor combines:

- A **direct-ink-written liquid metal force sensor** printed on a TPU substrate  
- A **flexible PCB (flex board)** for electrical interfacing and resistance readout  
- Encapsulation using thermoplastic polyurethane to form a robust and flexible device  

Such sensors are well suited for applications in **soft robotics, wearable systems, and pressure sensing**.

---

## Sensor Concept

- The force sensor consists of a **square spiral liquid metal conductor**.
- When a normal force is applied, the conductive channels are compressed.
- This compression reduces the cross-sectional area of the liquid metal trace, increasing its electrical resistance.
- The applied force is inferred from the measured change in resistance.

---

## Fabrication and Assembly Summary

The project involved the following major stages:

- Sensor layout design using PCB CAD tools  
- Direct-ink-write (DIW) printing of liquid metal traces on TPU  
- Soldering of electronic components onto a flexible PCB  
- Alignment and assembly of the printed sensor with the flex PCB  
- Encapsulation using hot pressing  
- Electrical and mechanical testing  

Multiple sensor variants with different trace spacings were fabricated and evaluated.

---

## Testing and Evaluation

Testing focused on characterizing force-sensing performance, including:

- Resistance response under incremental loading and unloading  
- Linearity and hysteresis behavior  
- Response time and measurement noise  
- Sensitivity and accuracy  
- Repeatability across multiple loading cycles
